Scope: AI4SG tools in healthcare and public health in **Bangladesh, India, Indonesia and Thailand**. Use together with the Healthcare General extraction rule (Sheet 1).

# **Variables to extract**

## **1. Study / Source**

## **2. AI Tool / Program**

## **3. Outcome Domain**

**Definition:**

Standardized category describing the type of outcome measured.

Use one of the following categories whenever possible.

### **Adoption**

Whether eligible users, patients, health workers, or facilities begin using the AI tool or AI-enabled service.

Examples:

- share of eligible patients consenting to AI-assisted screening,

- share of community health workers who activated a chatbot,

- share of facilities that started using the AI system,

- share of clinicians who began using decision support.

**Keywords / cues:**

adopted, take-up, consented, enrolled, activated, onboarded, started using, participation rate

### **Usage**

How much, how often, or how long the AI tool is actually used after access/adoption.

Examples:

- scans or images analysed per day,

- queries per health worker per week,

- active users,

- sessions,

- continued use,

- retention.

**Keywords / cues:**

usage, frequency, volume, queries, messages, scans, active users, logins, retention

### **Diagnostic / Predictive Accuracy**

Agreement between AI outputs and a reference standard in a real setting, or correctness of AI-generated health information.

Examples:

- sensitivity,

- specificity,

- area under the ROC curve,

- positive and negative predictive value,

- agreement with specialists,

- share of chatbot answers judged correct by experts.

**Keywords / cues:**

sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, agreement

### **Technical Performance / Data Quality**

Whether the AI system functioned as intended in routine conditions.

Examples:

- share of images rejected as ungradable,

- system downtime,

- processing or turnaround time of the AI,

- share of questions the system could not answer.

**Keywords / cues:**

ungradable, image quality, rejected, failure rate, downtime, latency, unanswered

### **Case Detection / Service Yield**

Changes in the number or proportion of cases found, tests performed, or referrals generated by the screening or care process.

Examples:

- cases detected,

- number needed to screen or test,

- confirmatory tests avoided,

- referral rate.

**Keywords / cues:**

case detection, yield, notification, number needed to screen, tests saved, referral rate

### **Care Continuum / Linkage**

Whether people move through the care pathway after the AI output.

Examples:

- referral completion,

- time to diagnosis,

- time to treatment initiation,

- treatment initiation rate,

- follow-up completion.

**Keywords / cues:**

linkage, referral uptake, loss to follow-up, time to treatment, treatment initiation, completion

### **Health Behaviour / Programme Engagement**

Changes in health-related behaviour, knowledge, or engagement with a health programme among patients or beneficiaries.

Examples:

- listening to programme voice messages,

- drop-off from an Health programme,

- medication adherence,

- health knowledge,

- care-seeking.

**Keywords / cues:**

engagement, care-seeking, behaviour, drop-off

### **Health Outcome**

Changes in clinical or population health status.

Examples:

- mortality,

- morbidity,

- low birth weight,

- vision loss,

- glycaemic control,

- treatment success.

**Keywords / cues:**

mortality, morbidity, clinical outcome, health status, incidence, cure, treatment success

### **Health-Worker Practice / Knowledge**

Changes in health-worker clinical behaviour, guideline adherence, decisions, or knowledge.

Examples:

- adherence to clinical guidelines,

- change in referral decisions,

- health-worker knowledge score,

- quality of counselling.

**Keywords / cues:**

guideline adherence, clinical practice, decision change, knowledge, counselling quality

### **Health-Worker Productivity / Workload**

Changes in health-worker time, workload, output, or efficiency.

Examples:

- patient encounters per hour per specialist,

- reading time per image,

- time saved,

- patients served per health worker.

**Keywords / cues:**

productivity, workload, time saved, throughput, encounters per hour, efficiency

### **Service Efficiency / Timeliness**

Changes in the speed or efficiency of the service from the patient's or facility's perspective.

Examples:

- waiting time,

- screening duration per patient,

- time to result,

- same-day results.

**Keywords / cues:**

waiting time, turnaround, duration, time to result, same-day

### **Patient Experience**

Satisfaction, trust, or acceptability reported by people who actually used the AI-enabled service.

Examples:

- patient satisfaction score,

- trust in AI result,

- willingness to attend referral.

**Keywords / cues:**

satisfaction, experience, trust, acceptability among users

### **Access**

Whether the intervention increases access to screening, diagnosis, treatment, or health information.

Examples:

- number of people screened in areas without specialists,

- coverage of eligible population,

- access in rural areas.

**Keywords / cues:**

access, reach, coverage, availability, underserved, rural

### **Equity**

Differences in adoption, usage, accuracy, or outcomes across groups.

Examples:

- older vs younger patients,

- women vs men,

- rural vs urban,

- people with vs without prior disease history,

- low-literacy vs higher-literacy health workers.

**Keywords / cues:**

heterogeneous effects, subgroup, disadvantaged, equity, disparity, performed worse among

### **Safety / Harms**

Adverse or unintended consequences of using the AI.

Examples:

- missed cases (false negatives) and their consequences,

- over-referral burden from false positives,

- inappropriate or unsafe advice,

- adverse events,

- privacy incidents.

**Keywords / cues:**

safety, harm, adverse event, false negative, missed, over-referral, unsafe, misinformation

### **Economic**

Economic-evaluation results reported as study outcomes.

Examples:

- cost per case detected,

- incremental cost-effectiveness ratio,

- cost savings to the health system.

**Keywords / cues:**

cost-effectiveness, incremental cost-effectiveness ratio, cost per disability-adjusted life year, cost per quality-adjusted life year, cost savings, budget impact

### **Administrative / System**

Changes in health-programme or health-system operations.

Examples:

- efficiency of allocating limited outreach calls,

- insurance-claims processing time,

- fraud detected,

- stock-out rates.

**Keywords / cues:**

allocation, targeting, prioritisation, claims, fraud, supply chain, operations

### **Other**

Use only when none of the above categories fit.

Explain clearly in the outcome name.

## **4. Outcome Name**

**Definition:**

Specific variable or result being measured.

The name should be more precise than the outcome domain.

**Examples:**

- Sensitivity for vision-threatening diabetic retinopathy

- Specificity for referable diabetic retinopathy

- Xpert tests avoided

- Specialist clinic productivity

- Reduction in engagement drop-off

- AI-screening consent rate

- Weekly chatbot queries per health worker

- Ungradable image rate

- Screening duration per patient

**Keywords / cues:**

dependent variable, primary outcome, secondary outcome, endpoint, measure

## **5. Outcome Measure**

**Definition:**

How the outcome was operationalized or measured.

**Examples:**

- AI output vs adjudicated grading by retina specialists,

- AI output vs Xpert MTB/RIF result,

- completed care encounters per hour per specialist from clinic records,

- share of automated voice messages listened to beyond a set duration, from call logs,

- number of WhatsApp queries per user from system logs,

- self-reported satisfaction scale.

Be concise.

**Keywords / cues:**

measured using, reference standard, constructed from, call logs, clinic records, survey scale, administrative records

## **6. Population / Sample for Outcome**

**Definition:**

The group for whom this particular outcome was measured.

This can differ from the overall study sample.

**Examples:**

- all patients screened,

- patients with gradable images only,

- patients with a reference-standard result,

- randomized beneficiaries,

- community health workers who used the chatbot,

- people aged over 60.

**Keywords / cues:**

analysis sample, gradable images, reference standard available, respondents, subgroup, users

## **7. Comparison**

**Definition:**

What the reported result is compared against.

**Possible answers:**

- Treatment vs control (randomized)

- AI-assisted care vs standard care

- AI vs human reader (e.g., radiologist, ophthalmologist)

- AI-alone vs AI-assisted human

- AI vs reference standard (accuracy; no comparison arm)

- AI threshold A vs threshold B

- Before vs after deployment

- Subgroup A vs subgroup B

- No comparison / descriptive outcome

**Example:**

Clinic days randomized to autonomous AI diabetic-eye exams vs clinic days without AI diagnosis (Bangladesh cluster-randomized trial).

**Keywords / cues:**

compared with, relative to, control, standard of care, reference standard, baseline, difference, treatment effect

## **8. Result Value**

**Definition:**

Main numerical estimate reported for the outcome.

Record the number only where practical.

**Examples:**

- 91.4

- 95.4

- 1.59

- 0.78

- -12

Do not include units in this field.

If several estimates are reported, use the principal specification (e.g., prespecified primary analysis or prespecified threshold) and explain alternatives in Result Notes.

**Keywords / cues:**

estimate, effect, sensitivity, specificity, AUC, rate, ratio, mean difference

## **9. Result Unit**

**Definition:**

Unit corresponding to the result value.

**Possible answers:**

- percent

- percentage points

- proportion

- AUC

- kappa

- SD

- per 1,000 screened

- encounters/hour

- minutes

- days

- number of cases

- risk ratio

- odds ratio

- hazard ratio

- currency

- currency per DALY or QALY

- other

**Example:**

encounters/hour

Do not confuse percentage points with percent.

## **10. Result Interpretation**

**Definition:**

Short plain-language description of the quantitative result.

**Example:**

On clinic days using autonomous AI, specialist productivity was 1.59 completed encounters per hour, compared with 1.14 on control days (about 40% higher).

Keep this factual and concise.

Do not add causal interpretation beyond what the study design and authors support.

## **11. Standard Error / Confidence Interval**

**Definition:**

Uncertainty measure reported with the estimate.

**Intended answer:**

Report either:

- standard error,

- confidence interval,

- or both if available.

**Examples:**

- SE = 0.07

- 95% CI: 87.1 to 95.0

If not reported:

**NR**

**Keywords / cues:**

standard error, SE, confidence interval, CI

## **12. P-value / Statistical Significance**

**Definition:**

Reported p-value or significance information.

**Intended answer:**

Prefer exact p-value where available.

Examples:

- p = 0.03

- p \< 0.001

- statistically significant at 5%

- not statistically significant

- not applicable (descriptive accuracy estimate)

If significance is shown only through stars, report the implied threshold if clearly defined by the paper.

**Keywords / cues:**

p-value, significance, \*, **,** \*

## **13. Measurement Timing**

**Definition:**

When the outcome was measured relative to the intervention.

**Possible answers:**

- At point of care (real-time AI result)

- End of a 7-week study period

- During the trial period (all randomized clinic days)

- End of a monsoon or programme season

- 6-month follow-up

- 1-year follow-up

**Keywords / cues:**

endline, follow-up, study period, post-intervention, months later

## **14. Subgroup**

**Definition:**

Whether the reported outcome applies to a specific subgroup rather than the full sample.

**Possible answers:**

- Full sample

- Age over 60

- People with prior tuberculosis

- Women

- Men

- Rural residents

- Low-income or low-literacy beneficiaries

- High-risk beneficiaries

- Gradable images only

- Other

If full sample:

**Full sample**

**Keywords / cues:**

heterogeneous effects, subgroup, by age, by sex, by risk group, stratified

## **15. Actual vs Intended Usage**

Use this field **only for Adoption or Usage outcomes**.

**Definition:**

Clarifies whether the result refers to:

- planned access,

- initial adoption,

- actual use,

- sustained use.

**Possible answers:**

- Offered access

- Consented / activated

- Used at least once

- Repeat use

- Average usage

- Sustained usage

- Compliance with protocol

**Example:**

Consented: 231 of 255 patients seen in the dispensaries (90.6%) agreed to AI-assisted diabetic-retinopathy screening.

This field is useful because "offered access" is not the same as "actually used."

## **16. Result Notes**

**Definition:**

Short clarification needed to interpret the outcome correctly.

Use for:

- AI threshold or operating point used,

- reference standard,

- whether ungradable images or failed AI reads were excluded from the denominator,

- prospective vs retrospective data,

- alternative specifications or thresholds,

- important denominator definitions,

- multiple related estimates,

- whether result is ITT or per-protocol,

- whether result comes from logs, clinical records, or self-reports,

- any ambiguity.

**Example:**

Sensitivity computed against adjudicated retina-specialist grading; ungradable images excluded; sensitivity at the WHO target-product-profile threshold reported separately.

Do not use this field for general paper commentary.

# **Special coding rules**

## **Adoption vs Usage**

Do not combine them automatically.

**Adoption** answers:

> Did the intended user begin using the tool?

**Usage** answers:

> How much or how often did the user actually use it?

Example:

- 90.6% of eligible patients consented to AI screening → Adoption

- Average of 40 scans per day per site → Usage

## **Access vs Usage**

Do not treat being offered access as actual usage.

Example:

- 1,000 ASHAs added to the chatbot → access

- 600 ASHAs sent at least one query → adoption

- average 3 queries per week → usage

## **Accuracy vs Health Outcomes**

High diagnostic or predictive accuracy is not evidence of improved case detection, treatment, or health. Record accuracy as Diagnostic / Predictive Accuracy.

If the study also measures case detection, linkage to care, or health outcomes, create separate outcome rows in the corresponding domains.

## **Sensitivity and specificity**

Record sensitivity and specificity (and AUC, PPV, NPV) as **separate rows** for the same target condition and threshold.

If several thresholds are reported, use the prespecified, manufacturer-recommended, or programme threshold as the principal estimate and describe others in Result Notes.

## **Image gradability and technical failures**

Record the share of images or cases the AI could not process as a Technical Performance / Data Quality outcome.

In Result Notes for every accuracy row, state whether these cases were excluded from the denominator.

## **Programme engagement vs health outcome**

If an AI system improves engagement with an mHealth programme (e.g., fewer beneficiaries dropping off voice-message listening), record it under Health Behaviour / Programme Engagement.

Do not treat engagement as equivalent to improved maternal or child health. Create a separate Health Outcome row only if health status was measured.

## **Prediction or prioritisation accuracy**

If an AI system predicts risk or prioritises beneficiaries, record prediction quality as Diagnostic / Predictive Accuracy or Administrative / System.

If the study also measures the effect of acting on the predictions, create a separate outcome row.

## **AI-alone vs AI-assisted human**

If a study reports both AI-alone performance and the performance of clinicians using AI, record them as separate rows with different Comparison entries.

## **Multiple conditions**

If a study separately reports results for different target conditions or definitions, create separate rows.

Example:

| **Outcome Domain**               | **Outcome Name**                                        |
|----------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|
| Diagnostic / Predictive Accuracy | Sensitivity for referable diabetic retinopathy          |
| Diagnostic / Predictive Accuracy | Sensitivity for vision-threatening diabetic retinopathy |

## **Multiple time periods**

If the same outcome is measured immediately and at longer follow-up, create separate rows when the distinction is important.

Example:

- Referral completion within 30 days

- Referral completion within 6 months

## **Data source of the outcome**

Record in Result Notes whether each outcome comes from system logs, clinical or programme records, independent assessment, or self-report. Developer-reported figures without an independent evaluation should be noted as such.
