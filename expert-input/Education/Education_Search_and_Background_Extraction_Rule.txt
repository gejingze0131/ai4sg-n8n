Education search and background extraction
Literature search and structured evidence extraction on AI for Social Good (AI4SG) tools in
the education sector in Bangladesh, India, Indonesia and Thailand.


1. Objective
Identify relevant empirical studies, evaluations, conference papers, working papers, government
reports, and credible institutional reports on AI-enabled agricultural tools that have been
actually tested, deployed, implemented, or used in the education sector in Bangladesh,
India, Indonesia, or Thailand.
Geographic scope: a source is eligible if the AI tool was tested or used in at least one of the four
countries. Multi-country studies are eligible only if results, sample sizes, or implementation
details for at least one of the four countries are reported separately; extract only the
country-specific information.

The purpose is to extract factual information that can later be used for analysis of:

   ●​    intervention
   ●​    adoption
   ●​    effectiveness
   ●​    implementation
   ●​    deployment
   ●​    scaling
   ●​    cost
   ●​    financing

Focus on collecting accurate and comparable information across studies.




2. Definition of AI4SG in education
For this review, define AI4SG in education as:

        The use of AI-enabled systems to address socially important educational problems
        by improving learning, access, equity, inclusion, teaching capacity, or the efficiency
        and quality of education delivery, especially where students, teachers, schools, or
        education systems face resource, capacity, or access constraints.

Do not require the paper itself to use the term "AI for Social Good."
Include an application when:

   1.​ AI is a substantive component of the tool or service;
   2.​ the application addresses an educational problem; and
   3.​ the intended outcome concerns learning, access, equity, inclusion, teaching capacity,
       affordability, or education-system performance.

Include, where relevant:

   ●​   intelligent tutoring systems
   ●​   personalized adaptive learning
   ●​   adaptive learning platforms
   ●​   generative-AI or LLM tutors
   ●​   educational chatbots
   ●​   teacher copilots
   ●​   AI-assisted teacher support
   ●​   automated feedback
   ●​   automated assessment
   ●​   AI-supported formative assessment
   ●​   dropout or learning-risk prediction
   ●​   early-warning systems
   ●​   AI for accessibility or special education
   ●​   AI translation or language-learning systems

Exclude ordinary digital education tools where AI is not a substantive component.




4. Type of evidence to prioritize
Prioritize sources that contain information on real interventions or real deployments,
including:

   ●​ randomized trials
   ●​ field experiments
    ●​   quasi-experimental evaluations
    ●​   implementation studies
    ●​   deployment evaluations
    ●​   scaling studies
    ●​   observational studies using actual usage data
    ●​   government evaluations
    ●​   international-organization reports
    ●​   credible program evaluations
    ●​   implementation reports
    ●​   cost or financing reports

Do not prioritize:

    ●​   opinion pieces
    ●​   purely conceptual papers
    ●​   general discussions of AI in education
    ●​   attitude-only studies
    ●​   intention-to-use surveys without actual usage data
    ●​   purely technical machine-learning papers without real educational deployment
    ●​   papers where AI is used only for generic convenience

6. Unit of Analysis and Cross-Source Linking

Use the AI intervention or deployment as the main unit of analysis, rather than the individual
paper or source.
A single AI tool may be used in multiple interventions or deployments, while multiple sources
may describe the same intervention or deployment. Assign unique IDs to identify these entities
and link related evidence across sources.

Assign three types of IDs:

   ●​ Tool ID: Identifies a distinct AI tool or system. Retain the same Tool ID when the tool is
      used across different interventions or deployments.
   ●​ Intervention ID: Identifies a distinct intervention or real-world deployment of an AI tool.
      Assign a new ID when the intervention design, intended application, or deployment
      setting differs materially.
   ●​ Source ID: Identifies each distinct paper, report, government document, webpage, or
      other source.

Cross-source identification and linking:

   ●​ Before assigning a new ID, check whether the AI tool or intervention has already been
      identified.
   ●​ Use tool/programme names, providers, implementing organizations, locations,
      implementation periods, target populations, and intervention designs to identify related
      records.
   ●​ When multiple sources describe the same intervention or deployment, retain the same
      Intervention ID and link all relevant Source IDs.
   ●​ When the same AI tool is used in different interventions or deployments, assign separate
      Intervention IDs while retaining the common Tool ID.
   ●​ Combine complementary information across related sources, including effectiveness,
      adoption, costs, implementation, financing, and scaling, while preserving the original
      source of each finding.
   ●​ Do not double-count the same intervention or outcome simply because it appears in
      multiple sources. Check whether different publications report the same underlying
      evaluation, sample, or results.
   ●​ If it is unclear whether two sources describe the same intervention, flag the relationship
      for verification rather than automatically merging the records.

Recording rule: Maintain one main extraction record per distinct intervention/deployment,
linked to its Tool ID, Intervention ID, and all relevant Source IDs.

10. Final output requirements
The main output should be a clean, verified evidence database that can later be used for
cross-study analysis.

The goal is:

      To collect comparable factual information on how AI education tools are
      designed, adopted, implemented, deployed, used, scaled, funded, and
      associated with educational outcomes.
